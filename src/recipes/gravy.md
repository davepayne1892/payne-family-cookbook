A rich, velvety slow-roasted onion gravy made by charring and oven-caramelising onions, blending them into a smooth puree, and reducing with wine, stock, and roasting juices. Pairs beautifully with roast chicken, pork, sausages, or a festive roast.

### Ingredients
- 5 brown onions
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- Fresh herbs (e.g. whole sage leaves, thyme, or rosemary)
- 50g butter
- 50g plain flour
- 200ml dry white wine (or swap for extra chicken stock)
- 500ml chicken stock
- 200ml gravy (prepared from granules such as Bisto, or extra stock)
- Meat roasting juices (optional, from roast chicken, pork, or sausages)
- 1 tbsp cranberry sauce (optional, great for Christmas)
- Sea salt and black pepper to taste

### Instructions
1. Preheat the oven to 150°C (300°F / gas 2). Line a deep baking tin or roasting tray with tin foil.
2. Chop the onions in half across the grain (so that each half has exposed rings). Remove only any loose outer skins, leaving the tight skins on where possible to keep the halves together.
3. Heat a splash of oil in a large frying pan over medium-low heat. Place the onions cut-side down into the pan with a few fresh herbs (whole sage leaves work brilliantly). Fry slowly without moving them until you have a deep, rich char on the surface and the concentric layers are starting to spread out.
4. Transfer the onions to the foil-lined tin with their charred cut surfaces pointing upwards as much as possible.
5. Sprinkle the brown sugar over the onions, scatter over more fresh herbs, and drizzle with a little oil so the sugar and oil seep down into the layers.
6. Loosely cover the tin with foil and bake low and slow for about 2 hours at 150°C, until the onions are completely tender, sweet, and caramelised throughout.
7. Remove from the oven and let cool slightly until safe to handle. Slip the softened onion flesh out of the skins, discarding the skins and root tips.
8. Place the warm onion flesh into a blender with a splash of cooking liquid (a little of the white wine or stock) and blend until completely smooth and pureed.
9. In a large saucepan over medium-low heat, make a roux: melt the butter, then stir in the flour. Cook, stirring constantly for 2 to 3 minutes, until it turns lightly golden and releases a nutty aroma.
10. Add the blended onion puree into the roux, stirring well and letting it cook out for 1 to 2 minutes.
11. Add the cooking liquids in order, treating the gravy like a risotto by stirring frequently and reducing down slightly after each addition: first pour in the 200ml white wine and reduce; next gradually whisk in the 500ml chicken stock; finally add the 200ml gravy (or stock) plus any roasting or resting juices from your meat.
12. Simmer gently until thick, glossy, and coating the back of a spoon. If you want an ultra-smooth finish, pass through a fine sieve. If making for Christmas, stir through a spoonful of cranberry sauce for extra depth and gloss. Season with sea salt and black pepper to taste.
